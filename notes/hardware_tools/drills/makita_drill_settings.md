# Makita drill settings

```
Created at: 2026-07-28
```

## Speed change lever

Right at the flat top of the drill, there is a speed change lever. This lever
allows you to switch between two speed settings: low (1) and high (2). The low
setting is ideal for driving screws and drilling into soft materials when you
need more precision and torque, while the high setting is better suited for
drilling.

## Clutch Settings

A series of numbers is printed around the clutch ring, which is located just
behind the chuck (where you put the drill bits and the screws). These numbers
represent the torque settings of the drill. The higher the number, the more
torque is applied, which is useful for driving larger screws or drilling into
harder materials. Lower numbers are better for delicate tasks to avoid
stripping screws or damaging materials.

Once the limit is reached, the drill will make a clicking sound, indicating
that it has reached its maximum torque for that setting. This feature helps
prevent overdriving screws and damaging the material.

Always start in the lowest gear, and as you need to drive the screw deeper or
drill into harder materials, you can increase the torque setting by turning the
clutch ring to a higher number.

## Modes

Some drills have a mode selector switch that allows you to choose between
different operating modes, such as drilling, driving screws, or hammer
drilling. Make sure to select the appropriate mode for your task to ensure
optimal performance and prevent damage to the drill or the material.
